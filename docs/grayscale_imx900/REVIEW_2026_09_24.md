# Recovery from the legacy-branch detour (2026-09-24)

The current implementation base is `active-sensing-grayscale-imx900`, commit
`37950e2`. The legacy `active-sensing-sim-minimal` commits `076050b` and
`f03533c` have not been merged or cherry-picked. Keep them as historical
D455 diagnostics only.

## Withdrawn interpretations

The earlier 0/6 fixed, 2/6 "detached", and 3/6 diffopt results do not establish
a sensor-gradient benefit. The so-called detached mode was a fixed initial
camera, the optimizer used privileged quality/geometry maps, and the controller
used a true gate location/visibility rule. Methods consumed different random
streams. Increasing the sample count does not repair these causal limitations.
The old script is not a deployment-capable visual policy or a navigation-loss
camera learner. Neither old experiment code nor its summary tool belongs here.

## Repairs on the grayscale branch

- The documented standalone renderer command now resolves project imports.
- Evaluation rejects batches larger than one, avoiding whole-batch termination
  on a single collision. It stops on goal arrival (collision takes priority),
  and includes the final state's clearance in the minimum-clearance metric.
- Each evaluation episode resets its RNG. Frame observation and dynamics have
  distinct reproducible seeds so camera RNG consumption and previous episode
  termination cannot shift later dynamics draws. This matches exogenous
  randomness, **not trajectories**: different policies should fly differently.
- `tools/evaluate_gray_vision_gate.py` compares a grayscale checkpoint, the same
  checkpoint with pixels zeroed, and an independently trained blind checkpoint.
  It records episode seeds, calibration, checkpoint hashes, source revision/diff,
  and paired bootstrap intervals. Intervals are conditional on the checkpoints,
  not uncertainty across training seeds.
- The old fixed E/G=0.35/0.15 saturates 97.1% of nominal static probe pixels under
  the current provisional profile. Both nominal vision-gate configs now use
  E/G=0.10/0.00: mean=0.383, saturation=0 in the same two-pose probe. No physical
  calibration coefficients were changed. This is not a hardware measurement.
- Training now saves local metrics, resolved arguments, and a final checkpoint
  even for budgets that do not land on the 200-iteration save interval.
- A detached/frozen-flight run with zero camera regularization may have no
  differentiable loss. It is a valid negative control; training skips backward
  instead of crashing in that case.

## Navigation-gradient wiring check

`tools/verify_gray_navigation_gradient.py` freezes an untrained flight policy,
sets camera smoothness weight to zero, and runs the real rollout/navigation loss
with identical model weights and seeded noise in both conditions. This is a
wiring check, not a successful policy experiment.

Observed navigation loss: 6.917368889 in both modes; forward trajectories match
exactly. Camera parameter gradient norm: full 0.005217741, detached 0.

Some CUDA operators in PyTorch 2.1 warn that deterministic implementations are
unavailable; matched RNG does not guarantee bitwise reproducibility on all
hardware or for complete training. The tested forward pair did match exactly.

## Fixed and classical camera stress probes

The nominal flight checkpoint was evaluated on 25 paired episodes per stress
scenario (seed range starting at 20000). Fixed settings demonstrate the needed
conflict:

| Fixed E/G | dark | bright | dark-to-bright | bright-to-dark |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.03 / 0.00 | 0% | 64% | 4% | 44% |
| 0.10 / 0.00 | 0% | 0% | 4% | 4% |
| 0.20 / 0.05 | 0% | 0% | 0% | 4% |
| 0.35 / 0.15 | 12% | 0% | 0% | 0% |

The high setting saturates 98.8% of bright-scene pixels; the low setting makes
100% of dark-scene pixels fall below the dark threshold. No fixed setting
dominates all conditions. Results are in
`logs/imx900_review/fixed_camera_sweep_3000`.

On the same nominal-trained flight checkpoint, mean AE reaches 12% overall
stress success and gradient AE 8%. Both improve dark to 32%, but distribution
shift still dominates navigation. These are diagnostic evaluations, not trained
classical baselines. A flight policy must first be trained under the mixed-light
adaptive-camera distribution. Results are in
`logs/imx900_review/classical_camera_3000`.

The learned camera previously started both its actuator state and sigmoid head
at 0.5/0.5, a strongly saturated operating point. Learned modes now initialize
the episode and camera head from the configured nominal E/G; full and detached
remain matched. Their configs use 0.10/0.02. The camera head weight is zeroed
and its bias is set with the inverse sigmoid, so the initial output is exact
and independent of random hidden features.

## Mixed-light mean-AE flight checkpoint

A flight policy was trained from scratch with mean AE across dark, bright,
dark-to-bright, and bright-to-dark conditions (3000 iterations, batch 64,
seed 42, AMP, W&B online). Its last 100 training iterations averaged 66.6%
success and 30.3% collision. On 100 held-out evaluation episodes, 25 per
scenario starting at seed 30000, it achieved:

| Scenario | Success | Collision | Mean exposure | Mean gain |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| dark | 56% | 36% | 0.6556 | 0.0613 |
| bright | 72% | 28% | 0.0456 | 0.0031 |
| dark-to-bright | 56% | 40% | 0.3787 | 0.0270 |
| bright-to-dark | 80% | 20% | 0.1434 | 0.0091 |
| overall | 66% | 31% | 0.3058 | 0.0251 |

Average saturation was 0.01% and average dark-pixel fraction was 1.92%.
This checkpoint is suitable as the common frozen-flight starting point for the
matched learned-full and learned-detached experiment. It is still one training
seed under the provisional camera profile; full/detached results require
multiple training seeds before a research claim.
